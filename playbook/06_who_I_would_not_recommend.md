# Who I Would Not Recommend Following and Why

## Purpose

One objective of this research was not only to identify valuable sources of information but also to evaluate their suitability for the type of SEO workflow documented in this playbook.

Choosing not to recommend an expert does **not** imply that their work lacks value or has not produced successful results. Instead, it reflects my assessment that their overall methodology is less aligned with the objectives, assumptions, and long-term philosophy adopted throughout this playbook.

---

# Julian Goldie

## Why I Would Not Recommend Following Him as a Primary Reference

Julian Goldie's work demonstrates impressive expertise in AI automation, workflow engineering, and scaling content production. His systems show how AI can significantly increase publishing speed while reducing manual effort through automation.

These ideas are valuable for understanding how AI can improve operational efficiency, and several aspects of his work informed my understanding of scalable AI-assisted workflows.

However, after comparing his recommendations with the broader body of research collected for this project, I chose not to adopt his methodology as the primary foundation for this playbook.

### My Reasons

**1. Greater emphasis on scale than editorial quality**

Julian's workflows prioritize automation and publishing efficiency. While this approach offers clear productivity benefits, I believe it places less emphasis on the structured human editorial oversight that was consistently reinforced by several other experts, including Ryan Law, Kevin Indig, and Lily Ray.

---

**2. Higher operational risk**

As AI-generated content becomes more common, I believe organizations face increasing risks related to factual accuracy, originality, and trust.

For that reason, I consider structured human review to be an essential part of sustainable content production rather than an optional quality-control step.

---

**3. Limited alignment with this playbook's philosophy**

Throughout this repository, I consistently recommend using AI to accelerate research, drafting, and operational efficiency while keeping humans responsible for strategic decisions, editorial judgment, and final approval.

Although Julian's workflows demonstrate how AI can dramatically increase productivity, I believe the long-term competitive advantage will come from combining AI with strong editorial processes rather than maximizing automation alone.

---

## What I Still Found Valuable

Despite my reservations, I found several aspects of Julian Goldie's work highly valuable:

- Building repeatable AI-assisted workflows.
- Reducing repetitive operational work.
- Demonstrating practical AI automation techniques.
- Encouraging experimentation with emerging AI tools.

These ideas influenced parts of my thinking, even though I ultimately adopted a different overall methodology.

---

# Final Reflection

Every expert included in this research contributed valuable insights that helped shape this playbook.

My decision not to recommend Julian Goldie as the primary reference for this workflow should therefore be interpreted as a difference in methodology rather than a judgment of expertise.

Overall, I found the strongest long-term approach to be one that combines AI-assisted productivity with human expertise, structured editorial processes, topical authority, and measurable business outcomes. That philosophy is reflected consistently throughout this playbook and guided the recommendations documented across all sections.

This evaluation reflects my assessment based on the objectives and scope of this playbook. Different organizations, industries, and business goals may reasonably lead to different conclusions about which methodologies are most appropriate.
