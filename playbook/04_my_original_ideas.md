# My Original Ideas

## Purpose

While this playbook is primarily based on research from experienced SEO practitioners, I also wanted to identify opportunities to extend the research through independent thinking.

The following ideas were developed after comparing multiple expert perspectives and identifying gaps that were not explicitly addressed in the collected sources. They have not been validated through experimentation but are presented as hypotheses that could improve AI-assisted SEO workflows.

**Originality Statement**

During my review of the collected LinkedIn posts, YouTube videos, and interviews, I did not encounter either of the workflow concepts described below. While both ideas build upon recurring themes in the research - such as AI-assisted content production, human editorial oversight, and operational scalability - the proposed workflows are my own extensions of those ideas rather than recommendations taken directly from any individual source.
---

# Original Idea 1

## Adaptive Editorial Review Based on Content Risk

### The Idea

Instead of requiring the same editorial review process for every AI-generated article, I propose assigning different review levels based on the expected business and reputational risk of the content.

For example:

| Content Type | Suggested Review Level |
|--------------|-----------------------|
| General educational articles | Standard editorial review |
| Product comparison pages | Senior editor review |
| Pricing pages | Marketing + product review |
| Medical, legal, or financial topics | Subject-matter expert review |
| Original research or statistical content | Full fact-check and editorial review |

The level of review could also increase automatically for articles containing technical claims, competitive comparisons, or information that could directly influence purchasing decisions.

### Why I Think It Could Work

Many experts in this research recommend combining AI with human editorial oversight, but they generally describe human review as a single workflow step.

I believe editorial effort should scale with content risk rather than treating every article equally.

This approach could:

- Improve operational efficiency.
- Allocate editorial resources where they create the greatest value.
- Reduce unnecessary review time for low-risk content.
- Improve factual accuracy for high-impact content.

### Limitations

This approach would require organizations to define clear risk categories and editorial standards. Different industries would likely require different review criteria.

For example, healthcare and financial organizations would require substantially stricter review standards than a B2B SaaS company publishing educational blog content.

# Original Idea 2

## AI Content Confidence Score

### The Idea

Before an AI-generated article enters editorial review, I propose generating an internal "Content Confidence Score" that estimates how much human intervention the article is likely to require.

The score would not measure SEO quality directly. Instead, it would estimate the reliability of the draft based on factors such as:

- Number of unsupported factual claims.
- Presence of statistics or external references.
- Amount of duplicated or generic language.
- Coverage of user intent.
- Readability and structural completeness.
- Degree of originality compared to existing published content.

Articles with lower confidence scores would automatically receive more extensive editorial review before publication.

The score could also be recalculated after editorial revisions, allowing teams to measure whether human review meaningfully improved the overall quality of the article over time.

### Why I Think It Could Work

The experts consistently emphasize the importance of human review, but none of the collected sources propose prioritizing editorial effort before an editor begins reviewing the article.

A confidence score could help marketing teams:

- Identify high-risk drafts earlier in the workflow.
- Improve consistency across larger editorial teams.
- Reduce time spent reviewing higher-quality drafts.
- Create measurable quality-control standards for AI-assisted content production.

Rather than replacing human judgment, the score would help editors decide where their expertise is most valuable.

### Limitations

The scoring methodology would require experimentation and continuous refinement. It should be treated as a decision-support mechanism rather than an automated approval system, since many aspects of content quality still require human judgment.

# Reflection

Both ideas extend a recurring theme found throughout the research: AI should improve the efficiency of content production without reducing the importance of human expertise.

Rather than introducing new SEO techniques, these ideas focus on improving the operational workflow surrounding AI-assisted content creation. I believe future competitive advantages in SEO will come not only from producing better content, but also from designing operational systems that enable marketing teams to consistently produce trustworthy, high-quality content at scale.
