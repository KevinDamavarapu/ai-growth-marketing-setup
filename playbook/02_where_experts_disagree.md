# Where Experts Disagree

## Purpose

One of the most valuable outcomes of this research was discovering that experienced SEO practitioners often recommend different approaches to solving the same problem.

Rather than treating every recommendation as equally applicable, I compared the evidence, evaluated the assumptions behind each viewpoint, and selected the approach that I believe is most appropriate for AI-assisted SEO content production in B2B SaaS environments.

The goal of this section is not to determine which expert is "right," but to explain why I adopted one recommendation over another.

---

# Disagreement 1

## Should AI generate complete articles or support a human-led editorial workflow?

### Author A – Ryan Law

Ryan Law advocates integrating AI throughout the content production process to improve efficiency while maintaining strong human editorial oversight. He recommends using AI to accelerate research, outlining, drafting, and repetitive tasks, but emphasizes that strategic thinking, factual verification, and editorial judgment should remain human responsibilities.

His approach treats AI as a productivity tool within a structured content workflow rather than an autonomous content creator.

**Reasoning**

- Improves content production efficiency without compromising quality
- Establishes repeatable editorial systems
- Reduces repetitive work while preserving human expertise
- Produces higher-quality content through structured review

**Sources**

- LinkedIn – *Schema Markup Alone Does Not Improve AI Citations*
- LinkedIn – *Using AI to Analyze Product Visibility Across a Large Content Library*
- Ahrefs Podcast – *How to Automate Blog Writing With AI From Keyword to Published*
- Repository references available in `references.md`

---

### Author B – Julian Goldie

Julian Goldie demonstrates highly automated AI-powered content workflows designed to maximize publishing speed and operational scalability. His approach incorporates multiple AI tools and automation pipelines to significantly reduce manual effort and increase content output.

Rather than emphasizing extensive editorial involvement, his methodology focuses on building scalable systems capable of producing large volumes of content efficiently.

**Reasoning**

- Enables rapid content production
- Increases publishing capacity
- Improves operational efficiency through automation
- Reduces manual effort across the content creation process

**Sources**

- LinkedIn – *AI SEO Workflow Automation*
- LinkedIn – *Building AI Content Systems*
- YouTube – *AI SEO Automation Workflow*
- Repository references available in `references.md`

---

## My Decision

I recommend Ryan Law's approach.

Although highly automated workflows can significantly increase productivity, I believe long-term SEO success depends on producing trustworthy, accurate, and genuinely valuable content. Human editorial oversight improves factual accuracy, strengthens information gain, and ensures that published content reflects genuine expertise rather than simply generating large volumes of AI-written text.

My recommended workflow therefore uses AI to accelerate content production while keeping humans responsible for strategy, editorial decisions, quality assurance, and final approval before publication.

# Disagreement 2

## Where should SEO teams prioritize their efforts as search becomes AI-driven?

### Author A – Koray Tuğberk Gübür

Koray Tuğberk Gübür argues that sustainable SEO success is built on strong topical authority and semantic depth. Rather than creating isolated pieces of content targeting individual keywords, he recommends developing interconnected content that comprehensively covers a subject and clearly establishes entity relationships.

His approach focuses on helping search engines understand expertise through semantic relevance, contextual completeness, and well-structured information architecture.

**Reasoning**

- Builds long-term topical authority
- Improves semantic understanding
- Creates stronger search relevance across related topics
- Produces sustainable rankings that are less dependent on individual keywords

**Sources**

- LinkedIn – *Liquid SERP and the Future of Runtime-Generated Search Interfaces*
- LinkedIn – *Freshness-Based Ranking, Historical Authority, and Query-Dependent Search Systems*
- YouTube – *He Created an SEO Methodology. Then Everyone Tried to Steal It*
- Repository references available in `references.md`

---

### Author B – Kevin Indig

Kevin Indig focuses on how search is evolving through AI-generated experiences such as AI Overviews and LLM-powered search engines. He argues that visibility increasingly depends on brand recognition, trust, entity understanding, and being referenced across multiple trusted platforms rather than relying solely on traditional organic rankings.

His approach emphasizes optimizing for discoverability wherever users interact with AI systems, including search engines, communities, and authoritative external platforms.

**Reasoning**

- Optimizes for AI-generated search experiences
- Increases brand visibility beyond traditional SERPs
- Improves discoverability across multiple platforms
- Reflects the changing behavior of modern search engines

**Sources**

- LinkedIn – *G2 Paid Profiles Receive More Citations*
- LinkedIn – *AI Models Mention Strong Brands More Than Publishers*
- YouTube – *SEO in the Age of AI | Kevin Indig on Google Overviews, E-Commerce & The Future of Search*
- Repository references available in `references.md`

---

## My Decision

I believe topical authority should remain the foundation of every SEO strategy, while AI visibility should be treated as an additional optimization layer rather than a replacement.

Without strong topical authority and high-quality content, there is little value in optimizing for AI visibility. Conversely, excellent content that exists only on a company website may miss opportunities to appear in AI-generated responses. Therefore, my recommended approach combines Koray's emphasis on semantic authority with Kevin's focus on AI discoverability to create a strategy that is both technically strong and future-oriented.

# Disagreement 3

## Should authority be built primarily on your own website or across external platforms?

### Author A – Matt Diggity

Matt Diggity highlights the growing influence of external communities, particularly Reddit, in AI-generated search results. His research demonstrates that discussions on trusted third-party platforms increasingly contribute to AI citations and recommendations.

Rather than relying exclusively on a company's own website, he encourages marketers to build credibility wherever their audience already participates.

**Reasoning**

- External platforms increasingly influence AI-generated answers
- Community discussions strengthen credibility
- Improves visibility beyond traditional search rankings
- Diversifies sources of organic discovery

**Sources**

- LinkedIn – *Reddit's Influence on AI Search Visibility*
- LinkedIn – *Monitoring AI Overview Visibility*
- YouTube – *SEO in the Age of AI Overviews*
- Repository references available in `references.md`

---

### Author B – Kevin Indig

Kevin Indig acknowledges the growing importance of external platforms but consistently argues that a company's own website should remain the primary source of expertise. He emphasizes creating authoritative, trustworthy content that establishes a strong brand and serves as the canonical source of information.

External mentions should reinforce—not replace—the authority built on owned content.

**Reasoning**

- Maintains complete ownership of content
- Strengthens long-term brand authority
- Creates a reliable source for AI systems to reference
- Supports sustainable SEO regardless of platform changes

**Sources**

- LinkedIn – *AI Models Mention Strong Brands More Than Publishers*
- LinkedIn – *G2 Paid Profiles Receive More Citations*
- YouTube – *SEO in the Age of AI | Kevin Indig on Google Overviews, E-Commerce & The Future of Search*
- Repository references available in `references.md`

---

## My Decision

I recommend treating the company website as the primary source of expertise while actively building supporting authority across trusted third-party platforms.

Publishing high-quality content on an owned website provides long-term control and establishes a reliable knowledge base. However, the research also indicates that AI systems increasingly incorporate signals from platforms such as Reddit, LinkedIn, and other trusted communities. Building authority across both owned and external channels therefore provides greater resilience and improves visibility in modern AI-assisted search experiences.

# Conclusion

The disagreements documented throughout this section demonstrate that experienced SEO practitioners often recommend different approaches based on their objectives, operating environments, and underlying assumptions.

Rather than adopting a single expert's methodology, I evaluated the available evidence, considered the strengths and limitations of each perspective, and selected the approaches that I believe are most appropriate for AI-assisted SEO content production in B2B SaaS environments.

As a result, this playbook represents an evidence-based synthesis rather than a reproduction of any individual framework. Every recommendation is supported by research, informed by critical evaluation, and documented with complete source references in `references.md`.
